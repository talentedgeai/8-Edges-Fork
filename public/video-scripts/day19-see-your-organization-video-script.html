The chart from eight months ago

Production
Dave to camera, tight single, warm light, no music
B-roll: a dusty org chart slide, boxes and lines, then a hallway of real people mid-work
Land "the real one" to camera, hold
CUT POINT

Ask to see your organization and you'll get a slide. Drawn for a board meeting months ago, wrong within weeks.

Who reports to whom, today? Who's carrying too much? And the harder question underneath: how are your people really doing? Not the version they say in meetings. The real one.

Most CEOs run on a stale chart and a gut feel. I did too.

0:40

Psychological safety

Production
Card: AMY EDMONDSON · PSYCHOLOGICAL SAFETY
Card builds: SPEAK UP WITHOUT PENALTY → PROBLEMS SURFACE SMALL → TEAMS LEARN FASTER
Land "you can measure whether the truth is reaching you" to camera
CUT POINT

Amy Edmondson's research at Harvard put a name on what separates the best teams: psychological safety. The belief that you can speak up, flag a problem, admit a miss, without it being held against you.

Teams that have it learn faster and perform better, because problems surface while they're still small. You can't demand safety. But you can measure whether the truth is reaching you, and you can keep the structure that carries it honest.

1:20

The living org chart

Production
SCREEN RECORD: log into Edge8 OS, open /team/org; blur candidate and staff names
SCREEN RECORD: start at the top of the chart and scroll down slowly, one branch at a time
SCREEN RECORD: click ONE person's card open and pause on their role and reporting line
SCREEN RECORD: open /admin, click into the dashboard assistant chat, and type a plain-English question about the team, for example: how many people are in each office?
SCREEN RECORD: hold on the assistant's answer appearing
CONFIRM SCREEN WITH DAVE: the survey results screen is not in the approved capture list; the /admin assistant stands in for the listening rhythm here
Cursor moves slowly; every click deliberate; no fast scrolling
CUT POINT

This is our org chart in 8 Edges. Not a slide. It's drawn live from the same database as everything else, so when a reporting line changes, the chart changes, because they are the same fact.

Click any person and you see the real structure around them: their role, their team, who they report to today.

And alongside the structure, we listen on a rhythm: short surveys, asked consistently, in a channel built for honest answers. The AI reads the responses and surfaces the patterns. The humans do what only humans should: act on them.
