The strategy nobody executes

Production
Dave to camera, tight single, warm light, no music
B-roll: a strategy deck on a boardroom screen, heads nodding
Cut to the same office weeks later: everyone busy in different tools
CUT POINT

Picture the day you presented the strategy. Heads nodded. Nobody disagreed.

Six months later, every team is busy. Sales is chasing what's easy to close. Ops is hitting a number nobody remembers choosing. And the strategy? It died somewhere between the conference room and the calendar.

This is the execution gap, and it is the default in most companies. Not because people don't care. Because clarity doesn't travel on its own.

0:50

It's a translation problem

Production
Card: RUMELT · GOOD STRATEGY BAD STRATEGY
Card builds: FAST · FREQUENTLY DISCUSSED · AMBITIOUS · SPECIFIC · TRANSPARENT (Sull & Sull, MIT Sloan)
Land "goals that live on paper do not live in your team's attention" to camera
CUT POINT

The research is clear on why this happens. Richard Rumelt showed most companies don't even have a strategy problem, they have a translation problem: direction is clear at the top, and it loses specificity at every layer below.

And MIT's Sloan review found the fix has a name: FAST goals. Frequently discussed. Ambitious. Specific. Transparent. The key word is frequently. Goals that live on paper do not live in your team's attention.

The goal tree in 8 Edges

Production
SCREEN RECORD: log into Edge8 OS, open /admin/edges/goals
SCREEN RECORD: start at the top: the annual strategy banner above the tree; hover it, do not click away
SCREEN RECORD: scroll slowly down the tree: company goals, then office goals beneath them
SCREEN RECORD: click ONE company goal open and follow its chain down to a named person and their agent
SCREEN RECORD: attempt is optional but powerful: show that a goal cannot be saved without a parent (the empty parent field blocking save)
Cursor moves slowly; every click deliberate; no fast scrolling
CUT POINT

Here's what that looks like solved. This is the goal tree in 8 Edges.

The strategy sits at the top of the page, one banner, always visible. Under it, the company goals. Under those, each office's goals. And at the bottom, a named person, and the agent working beside them.

Two things make this different from a slide. First, the system won't save a goal that doesn't connect to the goal above it. Nothing floats free. Second, the AI reads this tree every single day: every agent briefing, every prepared meeting, starts from these goals. Your people get alignment through conversation. Your AI gets it through context. Same tree, both halves.
