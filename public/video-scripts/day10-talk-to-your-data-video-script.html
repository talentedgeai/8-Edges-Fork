0:00

The two-day report

Production
Dave to camera, tight single, warm light, no music
B-roll: a spreadsheet export in progress; an email saying "circling back on this"
Land "already wrong" to camera
CUT POINT

You need one number. Which clients have open deals but no meeting in the last thirty days? Simple question.

So you ask someone, who asks someone, who exports a spreadsheet. Two days later the report arrives. And it's already wrong, because the company kept moving while you waited.

Now multiply that by every question you didn't ask because it wasn't worth two days. That's the real cost. Not slow answers. Unasked questions. So most leaders run on feel, not because they want to, but because asking is too expensive.

0:40

Ready means one home

Production
Card: STANFORD · ONLY 6% OF COMPANIES HAD AI-READY DATA
Card: READY = THE ANSWER HAS ONE PLACE TO LIVE
Land "you don't need a report, you need a question" to camera
CUT POINT

Here's why that report is so expensive. Your data lives in forty places, so answering means gathering, and gathering means people.

Stanford found only 6 percent of companies had data ready for AI. Ready means one thing: the answer has one place to live. That's what day eight bought us. And once it's true, you don't need a report. You need a question, in plain English, straight to the live database. No analyst in the middle. No translation layer.

This is the payoff of the boring work. Day eight, one database. Day nine, written-down workflows. The moment those two exist, every question becomes cheap.

1:20

Ask the database

Production
SCREEN RECORD: open /admin, let the dashboard load fully, hold two seconds
SCREEN RECORD: click into the assistant chat on the dashboard
SCREEN RECORD: type exactly "Which clients have open deals but no meeting in the last 30 days?" and press enter
SCREEN RECORD: hold on the answer as it appears, then scroll it slowly, names blurred
SCREEN RECORD: type a follow-up, "How many deals are open right now, by stage?", press enter, hold on the answer
Cursor moves slowly; every click deliberate; no fast scrolling
CUT POINT

This is the assistant in 8 Edges, sitting on our live database. I'll ask it the exact question. Which clients have open deals but no meeting in the last thirty days?

Watch the time. Seconds, not days. It found that because there's one place the answer lives.

A follow-up used to mean another meeting, another export, another two days. Now it's just the next sentence. And this isn't a special dashboard someone built for the demo. It's the live company, every table, every record, open to anyone on the team who asks.

And notice what I still do. I decide what to ask, and I decide what to do about it. The AI does the fetching. I do the judging. Two days of gathering just became a follow-up question.
