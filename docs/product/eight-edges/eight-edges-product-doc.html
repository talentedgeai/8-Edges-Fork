Eight Edges

The path from strategy to execution, which is now 50% human and 50% AI. Eight layers, each with a human half and an AI half, cascading from company strategy down to every executor: person or agent.

01 · Why

Strategy dies between the conference room and the calendar

Every operating system for small companies exists to solve one problem: direction set at the top fades as it moves through the organization. Teams fill the gap with whatever is urgent and measurable in front of them. The gap between what leadership intended and what actually happens widens week by week. This is not a motivation problem. It is a translation and rhythm problem.

EOS (Traction), Bloom Growth, and Ninety solve it with a trademarked vocabulary: Rocks, Scorecards, Level 10 Meetings. Underneath the branding, they are repackaging published research: OKRs, goal-setting theory, leading indicators, and meeting cadence. Eight Edges skips the middleman and builds directly on the sources. That is our own doctrine applied to ourselves: frameworks, not prompts. It was never the AI, it was the thinking.

And there is one thing none of those systems can do, because they were designed for all-human companies: they have no answer for the question that now defines leadership. What should AI do, and what should humans do? Eight Edges is built around that question. Every existing tool treats AI as a feature. Eight Edges treats it as half the workforce.

One line: Eight Edges is the operating system that keeps a company of people and agents pointed at the same goals, with the propagation, the rhythm, and the casting decisions enforced by software instead of memory.

02 · The name

Why "Eight Edges"

An edge is where two faces meet. Our edge is formed where the human plane and the AI plane converge; the sharper the alignment between them, the sharper the edge. The figure 8 is two loops joined at a crossover point: one loop human, one loop AI, meeting at the goals. Tip the 8 sideways and it is the infinity symbol: infinite leverage.

The arithmetic makes it structural: Four Offices × two halves (human + AI) = eight edges. The system's job is keeping all eight sharp and pointed at the same strategy. And the framework itself has eight layers. The company name stops being a name and becomes the architecture.

03 · The eight layers

Each layer is half human, half AI

No layer is fully specified until both halves are defined. The human half owns judgment and commitment; the AI half owns collection, propagation, and vigilance. This two-sided definition is the structural difference between Eight Edges and every predecessor.

1

Strategy

ANNUAL · ONE PAGE

Diagnosis, guiding policy, coherent actions (Rumelt's kernel). Where we play, how we win, what we will not do. One page, reviewed quarterly, rewritten annually.

HUMAN HALFSet direction. Make the bets. Decide what not to build.
AI HALFStrategy becomes standing context, injected into every agent's instructions. Agents cannot drift from a strategy they are required to read.
2

Goals

QUARTERLY · COMPANY GOALS

Objectives (qualitative direction) and Key Results (measurable outcomes, never activities), cascading Company → Office → Executor. Cascading is translation, not duplication: each level asks what it must deliver for the level above to succeed. A parent KR becomes a child Objective; the link is a foreign key, so no orphan goals can exist.

HUMAN HALFChoose the objectives. Commit to targets. Own the tradeoffs.
AI HALFLint every new company goal (outcome vs activity, cascade link intact, target vs baseline sanity). Inject current KRs into every plan, briefing, and standup.
3

Casting

QUARTERLY · PER KEY RESULT THE 50/50 LAYER

The layer no predecessor has. For every Key Result, an explicit delivery-mix decision: human-led, AI-led, or blended. Casting is a leadership act: you have always cast the best performer for the work; AI is another performer, evaluated on output. Recast at review time when the evidence says so.

HUMAN HALFMake the casting call. Every KR keeps one accountable human, even when agents execute.
AI HALFReport own capacity and fit. Flag KRs where automation could raise leverage, or where an AI-led KR is underperforming and should recast.
4

Metrics

WEEKLY · LEADING INDICATORS

A short list of numbers per Office. Every metric has a formula, an owner, a target, and a direction (is up good or down good). Leading indicators over lagging wherever possible.

HUMAN HALFDecide what to measure and what good looks like.
AI HALFPull the numbers from source systems (Stripe, QuickBooks, CRM, analytics). No self-reporting, no stale dashboards, ever.
5

Sync

WEEKLY · SAME AGENDA

The weekly heartbeat: metrics review, KR check, solve Issues. Goals show up in every sync because the cadence of conversation is what keeps goals alive.

HUMAN HALFDiscuss, decide, commit. The meeting is where judgment happens.
AI HALFPrepare the packet before the meeting: numbers, KR deltas, pre-diagnosed misses, proposed agenda. Humans walk in at the decision point, not the data-gathering point.
6

Issues

ROLLING

The pressure valve. Anything blocking a KR or breaking a metric goes on the list, gets diagnosed (goal problem, system problem, or execution problem), and gets solved in Sync, oldest-impact-first.

HUMAN HALFSolve. Choose the fix, own the follow-through.
AI HALFDetect and file: a metric misses target two weeks running, an agent opens the Issue with a first-pass diagnosis attached.
7

Reviews

QUARTERLY · PER EXECUTOR

Three questions per executor: what progress did we make, what did we learn, what do we adjust. Misses are data: diagnose whether the goal was wrong, the system was constrained, or execution slipped, before drawing conclusions about anyone. Agents get reviewed too; sometimes the adjustment is a better prompt, sometimes it is recasting the work to a human.

HUMAN HALFCoach and judge. GROW-structured conversations, informed by coaching profiles.
AI HALFCompile the evidence packet: KR trajectory, patterns since last review, prepared coaching questions. Accurate information instead of general impressions.
8

Playbooks

EVERGREEN

Documented ways of working. A playbook is not done until it exists in both forms: human-readable (the doc) and agent-executable (the skill/workflow). One way of working, two runtimes.

HUMAN HALFAuthor the way of working; decide what deserves to be a playbook.
AI HALFExecute the playbook as a skill. Flag drift when reality stops matching the doc.

04 · The cascade

Company → Office → Executor

Three levels, and the third is the change: the cascade ends at Executors, not persons. An executor is a person, an agent, or a pair. Strategy translates downward until it reaches something that can do work, and half of those somethings are now AI.

Propagation is enforced by three mechanisms:

  1. Structural. Every child Objective links to a parent Key Result (a literal foreign key). Walking the chain from any individual goal reaches a company goal. Orphan goals are rejected at write time.
  2. Rhythmic. Goals appear in every weekly Sync, every 1-on-1 prep, every agent briefing, automatically. The FAST insight: frequently discussed goals drive behavior, filed goals do not.
  3. Contextual. Humans get alignment through conversation; agents get it through context injection. Same tree, two propagation channels. This is the 50/50 path from strategy to execution.

Worked example (Edge8 AI, Q3 2026, illustrative numbers)

COMPANY · STAFFING LINE O: Own the renewal base.
KR1: Renew 4 of 4 staffing contracts (Unlock, On Target, EO, Wareease).  KR2: Add 2 new staffing clients.  KR3: Every renewal proposal live within 48h of trigger date.
OFFICE OF REVENUE  ← translates company KR3 into its objective O: Make renewals a system, not a scramble.
KR1: Renewal workflow runs end-to-end for all 4 anniversaries.  KR2: Time from call transcript to live proposal under 10 minutes.
EXECUTOR  ← office KR2 becomes the executor's objective Dave (human, accountable) + developer agent (AI, executing)
O: Proposal generation fully agent-run.  KR1: crm-call-to-proposal playbook executes in under 10 min, 100% of runs.  KR2: Zero manual CRM writes for renewals this quarter. AI-LED

Notice the mechanics: the company KR became the office Objective; the office KR became the executor Objective. Each level did real translation, and the chain is traceable in the database.

06 · Boundaries

What Eight Edges is not

  • It is not Human Tokens. Human Tokens track building and client delivery (hours of skilled, leveraged work; AI Token Ratio). Eight Edges tracks company goals down to individuals and agents. Two separate ledgers; an executor goal may reference delivery outcomes, but the systems do not merge.
  • It is not a project manager. Tasks, tickets, and delivery live where they live. Eight Edges holds direction, numbers, and rhythm, and points everything else at them.
  • It is not EOS. No licensed vocabulary, no rocks. Plain words: Strategy, Goals, Casting, Metrics, Sync, Issues, Reviews, Playbooks.
  • Governance default: every Key Result has exactly one accountable human, even when AI-led. Agents execute and co-own; accountability never delegates to software.

08 · Rollout

Eat it first, then teach it, then sell it

  1. Dogfood (Q3 2026). Edge8 runs Q4 planning in Eight Edges: real strategy page, real company goals for both lines, casting on every KR, weekly Sync on the agent-prepared packet.
  2. Instrument (Q4 2026). Metrics agents pull weekly numbers; Issues auto-file; the 7am plan derives from KRs. FAST becomes ambient.
  3. Teach (2027). Eight Edges becomes the worked example inside Leadership in the AI Era and the AI Officer curriculum; the ADKAR rollout playbook ships with it.
  4. Sell (when proven). Client-facing Eight Edges: the operating system for companies that are half human, half AI, with our own numbers as the case study.

Decisions taken in this draft (flag to revisit): Casting is a full layer, not a field. Every KR keeps one accountable human. Playbooks require both human-readable and agent-executable forms. Engagement pulse (Gallup Q12) deferred to a later phase.
