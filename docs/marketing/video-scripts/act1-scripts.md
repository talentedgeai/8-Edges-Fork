## Day 02 · The Execution Gap

### 0:00 · The strategy nobody executes
Picture the day you presented the strategy. Heads nodded. Nobody disagreed.
Six months later, every team is busy. Sales is chasing what's easy to close. Ops is hitting a number nobody remembers choosing. And the strategy? It died somewhere between the conference room and the calendar.
This is the execution gap, and it is the default in most companies. Not because people don't care. Because clarity doesn't travel on its own.

### 0:50 · It's a translation problem
The research is clear on why this happens. Richard Rumelt showed most companies don't even have a strategy problem, they have a translation problem: direction is clear at the top, and it loses specificity at every layer below.
And MIT's Sloan review found the fix has a name: FAST goals. Frequently discussed. Ambitious. Specific. Transparent. The key word is frequently. Goals that live on paper do not live in your team's attention.

### 1:35 · The goal tree in 8 Edges
Here's what that looks like solved. This is the goal tree in 8 Edges.
The strategy sits at the top of the page, one banner, always visible. Under it, the company goals. Under those, each office's goals. And at the bottom, a named person, and the agent working beside them.
Two things make this different from a slide. First, the system won't save a goal that doesn't connect to the goal above it. Nothing floats free. Second, the AI reads this tree every single day: every agent briefing, every prepared meeting, starts from these goals. Your people get alignment through conversation. Your AI gets it through context. Same tree, both halves.

### 2:30 · Close
Strategy that travels. Goals your team actually discusses, and your AI actually reads. That's how execution stops being a gap.
Tomorrow: why your AI can't help you yet, and it starts with where your data lives. See you then.

## Day 03 · Your Business Lives in 40 Places

### 0:00 · The forty places
Quick audit. Where does your business actually live?
Customers in the CRM. Except the newest ones, they're in someone's inbox. Pricing in a spreadsheet. The real pricing in a different spreadsheet. People data in an HR tool, the books in accounting software, decisions in chat threads nobody can search.
Most companies run on about forty disconnected places. And then they ask AI to help. With what? AI can only create value where it can see the data.

### 0:45 · The 6 percent
When Stanford studied enterprise AI deployments, they found only six percent of companies had data that was actually ready for AI. Six percent. That's the club that wins, and the entry fee is one decision: one home for every fact, and a rule your whole company lives by: if it's not in the system, it didn't happen.

### 1:20 · One database in 8 Edges
Here's what one home looks like. This is our company, live.
Every company we work with, on one screen. Open one, and everything is already there: the people, the deals, the meetings, the invoices. Not five tools stitched together. One record.
And because it's one database, watch what the AI can do. I just ask, in plain English: which clients have open deals but no meeting in the last thirty days? And it answers. Across all of it. Instantly. That question used to take three people and two days.

### 2:25 · Close
Your AI is only as smart as what it can see. Give it one home for every fact, and it stops being a toy and starts being a colleague.
Tomorrow: the 77 percent, why most AI pilots die, and the one workflow that shows you the way out.

## Day 04 · Why AI Pilots Fail

### 0:00 · You bought the tools. Nothing changed.
Last year you bought the AI tools. Copilots, subscriptions, a pilot project with a nice deck.
Be honest: what changed? For most companies, the answer is nothing. The pilots demo well, and then they die quietly, and everyone concludes AI isn't ready.
AI is ready. The company isn't. And there's hard data on exactly why.

### 0:40 · The 77 percent, unpacked
Stanford studied fifty one successful AI deployments. Not the failures, the winners. And in seventy seven percent of them, the hard work was not the AI technology. It was the data, and redesigning the workflows.
Think about what that means. The companies that win at AI spend most of their effort on two unglamorous things: getting the data into one place, and writing down who does what. A workflow is designed when every step names its owner: a person, or an AI.

### 1:30 · A designed workflow, end to end
So let me show you one designed workflow, start to finish, in our company.
A sales call just ended. The transcript goes into the system. That's the last thing a human types.
The AI reads the call, updates the client record, moves the deal, and drafts the proposal. And there it is: a live proposal page, on our domain, ready to send. Under ten minutes from hanging up the phone.
Every step of that workflow has an owner. The human owns the call and the price. The AI owns everything in between. That's what the seventy seven percent buys you.

### 2:35 · Close
Your pilots didn't fail because the AI was weak. They failed because nobody gave it a designed workflow on real data. Fix those two things and the same AI becomes a machine.
Tomorrow: why you should stop writing clever prompts, and what to feed the machine instead.

## Day 05 · Frameworks, Not Prompts

### 0:00 · Mediocrity at scale
Everyone in your company is prompting right now. And almost all of them are producing the same thing: the polished average of the internet.
Type "act as a marketing expert" and you get what everyone else gets. That's not productivity. That's mediocrity at scale.
It was never the AI. It was the thinking you fed it.

### 0:40 · Point at a framework
Here's the upgrade. Instead of a clever prompt, point the AI at a battle-tested framework.
Say: use Jobs to Be Done, Clayton Christensen's framework from Harvard, to build this customer persona. Everything changes. The AI stops averaging the internet and starts thinking with decades of tested business science.
And the best part: you don't need to know how the framework works. You just need to know it exists. The AI knows it cold.

### 1:25 · Frameworks installed, not typed
Now, one level higher. In 8 Edges, the frameworks aren't typed, they're installed.
Watch: every resume that arrives gets screened against the job's actual criteria, the same structured rubric, every candidate, every time. Nobody prompts it. It's built in.
Every manager walks into their one-on-one with a brief the AI prepared using GROW, the coaching framework used by professional coaches for thirty years. Nobody prompted that either.
That's the difference between a company that prompts and a company that runs on frameworks. One depends on whoever typed best today. The other is consistent by design.

### 2:20 · Close
Stop polishing prompts. Install the thinking once, and let every person and every agent inherit it.
Tomorrow: the question that now defines leadership, what should AI do, and what should your people do?

## Day 06 · The 50/50 Question

### 0:00 · The question nobody can answer
Ask any leadership team this question and watch the room go quiet: what exactly should AI do in our company, and what should our people do?
Half of them fear giving AI too much. The other half fear falling behind. So most companies answer by not answering, and the decision gets made by default, one shortcut at a time.

### 0:35 · Leadership is 50% human, 50% AI
Brooks Holtom at Georgetown puts it plainly: leadership in this era is fifty percent human, fifty percent AI. Not as a metaphor. As a management practice.
You've always cast your best performer for each piece of work. AI is another performer. You evaluate it on output, you give it the work it's best at, and you keep a human accountable for the result. Choosing who does what is not a technical decision. It's a leadership act.

### 1:20 · The split, on screen
Here's what the fifty fifty looks like when it's real, not a slide.
A candidate comes in. The AI's score and reasoning sit on the left. The recruiter's own judgment sits right beside it. And when they disagree? The human wins, and the system records the disagreement and learns from it.
Same rule at the goal level. Plenty of goals in our tree are AI-led. Every single one still carries exactly one accountable human name. Agents execute. Accountability never delegates to software.

### 2:15 · Close
What should AI do, what should your people do. Stop leaving it to default. Decide it, write it down, and hold one human accountable for every outcome.
Tomorrow, Act One closes: the full plan, and where a real company actually starts.

## Day 07 · The Plan

### 0:00 · So where do you start?
This week we diagnosed it all. The strategy that doesn't travel. The data in forty places. The pilots that die. The prompts that average. The question nobody answers.
So now the only question left: where do you actually start? And here's the answer you'll be relieved to hear: not with a re-org.

### 0:35 · Four outcomes, four offices
Start with what you're trying to achieve. Four outcomes: greater revenue, higher performing talent, streamlined operations, and a culture that innovates. We call this the Four Offices of the Future: a way to organize the work so AI can help, without reorganizing your business.
And change lands in a sequence. The ADKAR research says people move through awareness, desire, knowledge, ability, reinforcement. This week was awareness. The next three weeks are knowledge and ability: we build, one office at a time.

### 1:25 · The map, on screen
Here's the map we'll follow.
One system. Four offices in the sidebar. Revenue: the deals, the calls, the proposals. Talent: the hiring, the onboarding, the coaching. Operations: the requests, the equipment, the books. Innovation: the ideas and the workflows they become.
And above them all, the goal tree, so every office, every person, and every agent stays pointed at the same strategy.
Next week we start where the money is: the Office of Revenue. One workflow per day. Real screens, real data, real results.

### 2:30 · Close
You know the strategy. By the end of this season, so will the other one hundred ninety nine, and half of them won't be human.
Act One is done. Tomorrow, we build. See you there.
